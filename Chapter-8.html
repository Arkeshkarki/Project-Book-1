Unit: Information Security and Cyber Law

Introduction

Information Security refers to the practice of protecting digital information from unauthorized access, misuse, modification, or destruction. Cyber Law is the legal framework that governs the use of the internet, digital communication, and online activities.

Objectives

  • To understand the importance of securing information.
  • To learn about threats and preventive measures.
  • To know the legal aspects of using computers and the internet.

Information Security

Definition

Information Security is the process of safeguarding data and information systems against unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, and destruction.

Principles of Information Security

  • Confidentiality: Ensuring that information is accessible only to authorized users.
  • Integrity: Protecting information from being altered or tampered with.
  • Availability: Ensuring that information and systems are available when needed.

Types of Threats

  • Viruses and Malware
  • Phishing Attacks
  • Unauthorized Access (Hacking)
  • Data Theft
  • Denial of Service (DoS) Attacks

Preventive Measures

  • Use of Antivirus and Firewalls
  • Strong Passwords and Authentication
  • Regular Software Updates
  • Data Encryption
  • User Awareness and Training

Cyber Law

Definition

Cyber Law refers to the set of legal rules and regulations that govern the use of computers, networks, and the internet. It deals with issues like cybercrime, intellectual property, privacy, and electronic transactions.

Importance of Cyber Law

  • Protects individuals and organizations from cybercrime.
  • Provides legal recognition to electronic documents and transactions.
  • Ensures privacy and data protection.
  • Defines punishments for misuse of technology.

Examples of Cyber Crimes

  • Hacking and Unauthorized Access
  • Identity Theft
  • Online Fraud and Scams
  • Cyber Bullying
  • Software Piracy

Cyber Law in Nepal

In Nepal, cyber law is governed by the Electronic Transaction Act (ETA) 2006. It provides legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures, and defines punishments for cybercrimes.

Advantages of Information Security and Cyber Law

  • Protects sensitive information.
  • Reduces risk of cyber attacks.
  • Ensures trust in online transactions.
  • Provides legal remedies against cybercrime.

Disadvantages / Challenges

  • High cost of implementing security measures.
  • Constantly evolving cyber threats.
  • Lack of awareness among users.
  • Difficulty in enforcing laws across borders.

Practical Work

  • Study common cyber threats and their prevention.
  • Practice creating strong passwords.
  • Explore Nepal’s Electronic Transaction Act (ETA) 2006.
  • Discuss real-life cases of cybercrime and their legal outcomes.
